## 1. Overview

Track A's purpose has been to develop, and then repeatedly test against the actual published p-DERS paper, a formal account of how safety guarantees that hold at one node of a distributed multi-agent system can be shown (or shown to fail) to compose into a guarantee about the whole system - "local correctness does not guarantee global safety" being the paper's own opening problem statement. The work proceeded in the order it was actually commissioned: first defining a causal-aware local projection (`pi_i^causal`), then attempting an "Overlap Consistency" result from scratch, then discovering the real paper existed and re-grounding everything in its actual definitions, then investigating a specific unresolved provenance question about the paper's notation, then re-revising the overlap-consistency work once the real paper's text was available, then attempting the genuine multi-node theorem the paper itself never states, and - following a Decision Ledger checkpoint (`DC_20260730_001`) and explicit further Human Gate authorization - formalizing a third, independent design pattern (Sound Local Approximation) that had been named but not developed in the compositional-safety document. This document is the second point where the whole arc is summarized in one place; v1 summarized the first six documents, this version adds the seventh and updates everything downstream of it.

Nothing below is new analysis. Every claim in Section 2 is a compression of something already stated, with its proof/non-proof status, in one of the seven source documents; Section 3's summary sentence follows the framing supplied by the instructing memo for this update; Section 4 only re-lists open items already recorded elsewhere, plus the three new items this update's instructing memo specified; Section 5 updates the status of the pending decision points named previously and adds the one new point specified.

## 2. Document-by-Document Summary

**1. `docs/formal/pDERS_causal_projection_v0.1.md`** - Formalized the upgrade from a plain coordinate projection `pi_i` to a causal-order-preserving `pi_i^causal`, presenting three candidates without recommending one: Candidate A (Lamport clock - cheap, but forces concurrent transitions into an arbitrary total order, failing the "don't force an order on concurrency" property), Candidate B (vector clock - preserves true concurrency exactly, at O(n) metadata cost, requires a bounded/known node set), and Candidate C (explicit causal-slice/DAG - most expressive, supports full audit/replay, but has no defined bound on how far back its history grows). Also laid out, without resolving, how a causal-metadata-bearing projection interacts with the (at the time still-uncommitted) local relation `R_i` and local invariants `Omega_i`/`Psi_i`, and listed the properties (P1-P7) a later Overlap Consistency argument would need. Human Gate's decision, recorded in the next document, adopted Candidate B and deferred Candidate C pending a horizon/pruning policy that was never separately defined.

**2. `docs/formal/references/pders_main_reference.md`** - A verbatim transcription of the actual p-DERS paper ("Projective Dual-Execution Refinement Systems... Formally Enforcing Safety Governance in Asynchronous Multi-Agent Orchestration"), obtained via an R02 web session upload and relayed into this session's instructing memo as text (kuroko's own session has never read the PDF directly). Establishes the paper's real formal apparatus: `R_i` (local simulation relations), `Omega_i`/`Psi_i` (Definitions 1-2, Node Safety Invariant and Transition Safety Invariant), the "Local Invariant Gate" acceptance rule combining all three, Assumptions 1-4 (with Assumption 4, Projection Consistency, being the paper's own analogue of "Overlap Consistency"), Lemmas 1-3, Theorem 4 (Decentralized Safety Preservation), Corollary 5, and the Adversarial Model's four capabilities (C1 Partial Observability, C2 Causal Reordering, C3 Semantic Composition, C4 Autonomous Behavioral Drift). Carries an explicit provenance/reliability caveat (second-hand transcription, not machine-read by this session) and a flagged list of specific notation spots that could plausibly be transcription artifacts, left unresolved rather than guessed at.

**3. `docs/formal/pDERS_overlap_consistency_v0.1.md`** - Written before the real paper was available, this built an "Overlap Consistency" proposition and a from-scratch definition of "globally safe" entirely from first principles, on top of Candidate B. Lemma 1 (Clock Agreement on Overlap) was fully proven: under Candidate B, with a static node index set, two nodes cannot disagree about the order or concurrency of events in their shared history, because a vector clock's value is a property of the event, not of the observer. Lemma 2 (Local-to-Global Bridge - the attempt to show local safety plus clock agreement implies no jointly-invisible violation) was left explicitly unproven, with four named missing prerequisites: the actual content of `R_i`/`Omega_i`/`Psi_i` (unknown at the time), the choice between two compatibility modes (M1: local relations blind to causal metadata, vs. M2: causal-aware), an explicit merge/conflict semantics for concurrent updates, and a working definition of "globally safe" itself.

**4. `docs/formal/pDERS_Ri_Omega_Psi_source_investigation_v0.1.md`** - A dedicated investigation into where a canonical formal source for `R_i`/`Omega_i`/`Psi_i` might exist, prompted by discovering that MoCKA's own AAAI2027 paper (a different paper, Formal Core `S_DTS=<E,P,V>`) does not contain these symbols at all. Exhaustively searched this repository, the full Decision Ledger (187 entries), events.db, and the Notion workspace, and found the symbols nowhere internally except this Track A series' own prior output. Found one authentic scrap of the real p-DERS preprint's own promotional language (a 2026-06-14 announcement) describing a single `pi_i` projection and a single, unnamed "Local Invariant Gate" - raising, without resolving, the question of whether the three-way `R_i`/`Omega_i`/`Psi_i` split was genuine paper notation or a later gloss. Concluded "unresolved" rather than a flat yes/no: confirmed absent internally, but external access to the actual Zenodo preprint was blocked by network policy. This was subsequently settled by Document 2 above: `R_i`/`Omega_i`/`Psi_i` do exist in the real paper, exactly as Definitions 1-2 and the Local Simulation Relations section state.

**5. `docs/formal/pDERS_overlap_consistency_v0.2.md`** - Once the real paper was available, this replaced Document 3's from-scratch construction with a grounded reading of the paper's own Assumption 4 and Theorem 4, adopting Theorem 4's statement directly as the target property rather than continuing to use an independently-invented definition of "globally safe." Found that Theorem 4's transcribed proof is single-node throughout - it lists Assumption 4 among its hypotheses but never visibly uses it, while the paper's multi-node composition story appears only in a figure description and in Corollary 5's phrasing, never as a stated theorem. Showed, via a type-correct counterexample (`Omega_i` identically 1, `Omega_j` identically 0), that Assumption 4 does not follow automatically from Assumptions 1-3, and split its provability into two branches: Branch 1 (`Omega_i`/`Psi_i` derived from one shared design-time policy) is provable conditionally, because C1/C2 are runtime threats that do not touch a static, offline property; Branch 2 (independently-authored per node) is not provable from the paper's own material, because C1 blocks any node from unilaterally verifying it and the paper's only related mechanism (the Async Drift Compensator) is asynchronous and post-acceptance, conflicting with the paper's own pre-mutation Security Objective. Also clarified that the paper's existing vector-clock component (the Formal Trace Recorder) sits after acceptance for audit purposes, distinct in both position and purpose from Track A's own Candidate B proposal to embed causal metadata into the synchronous admission path itself.

**6. `docs/formal/pDERS_compositional_safety_v0.1.md`** - Attempted the genuine multi-node theorem the paper itself never states, building directly on Document 5's Branch 1/Branch 2 split. For Branch 1, sharpened the informal "derived from a shared policy" idea into two separate, necessary conditions - Factorization (a single global invariant that does not distinguish states any one node's projection collapses together) and Coherent Overlap (any two nodes' shared observation genuinely traces back to one common global state, not merely two independently-existing preimages) - and proved, completely, that these two conditions together deliver Assumption 4 as a derived consequence (Lemma A), closing both of Document 3's Lemma-2 failure modes when combined with Document 3's own already-proven Lemma 1. The full Compositional Safety Theorem nonetheless stops at a named, unproven Lemma B: pairwise-compatible local traces (state agreement plus causal-order agreement) do not, on their own, guarantee that one single global witness trace exists realizing every participating node's history simultaneously - a lifting/completeness property of the projection family that is not implied by Assumptions 1-3 and was not supplied. For Branch 2, formally evaluated three candidate remediation mechanisms without selecting a winner: a synchronous cross-node verification gate (theoretically sufficient, but reintroduces exactly the global-synchronization cost the paper's design was built to avoid), pairwise onboarding compatibility contracts (C1-robust, but scales poorly and tends to converge back toward Branch 1 as the node count grows), and a newly-named third option, Sound Local Approximation (each node independently proven to never accept a state a single published reference policy would reject, requiring no runtime coordination and no pairwise checks, but delivering a strictly weaker guarantee than Assumption 4's literal equality, and leaving Lemma B untouched regardless of which Branch-2 mechanism is used).

**7. `docs/formal/pDERS_sound_local_approximation_v0.1.md`** - Took Document 6's third, unselected Branch-2 candidate and developed it fully, under explicit Human Gate authorization following the `DC_20260730_001` checkpoint. Formalized its four claimed properties (a single, published, versioned reference; a per-node soundness proof that never mentions any other node; zero change to the paper's own runtime Local Invariant Gate; scalability to a changing node set) and proved three genuinely new results: Lemma S1 (Join-Monotonicity - adding a node costs nothing for existing nodes' proofs, provided the reference itself has not changed), Lemma S2 (Update Monotonicity - soundness survives a reference *weakening* automatically, but a concrete counterexample shows it does *not* survive a *strengthening* without re-verification), and a Local Upgrade Proposition (Sound Approximation lifts Theorem 4's own per-node conclusion from "safe with respect to my own predicate" to "safe with respect to the one true published reference," using no additional machinery). The document's central, corrective finding is a repositioning: **Sound Local Approximation is not a point on the Branch 1/Branch 2 axis and not a solution to the Branch 2 problem** - it is a third, independent axis, answering a different question (is each node individually correct against one ground truth) than the one Branch 2's node-disagreement problem asks (do two independently-defined nodes agree with each other). Two nodes can each be individually sound and still disagree about a shared observation without either being wrong, so Sound Local Approximation does not even reach the point Lemma A's proof starts from (pairwise agreement), and it leaves Lemma B (global witness existence) completely untouched. Section 4 of that document maps this pattern, purely conditionally (properties C1-C4, "if...then" only), onto MoCKA's Governance Function `G`, asserting nothing about whether MoCKA's actual implementation satisfies any of those conditions.

## 3. Current State in One Paragraph

(2) Branch 2 remains unprovable; only one of its three remediation candidates (Sound Local Approximation) has been examined so far, and that examination showed Sound Local Approximation is not itself a solution to the Branch 2 problem - so, in substance, Branch 2's actual node-disagreement problem remains as unaddressed as it was when Document 6 first identified it. ## 4. Consolidated Open Questions

Collected from each document's own Open Questions (or, for Documents 2 and 4, their equivalent flagged-uncertainty / next-step sections) and grouped by recurring theme. Items A-M are unchanged from v1 (carried forward verbatim, since nothing in the new source document altered their status except where noted); items N-P are new, added per this update's instructing memo.

**A. Dynamic node membership (static `N` assumed throughout).** First raised in Document 1; sharpened in Document 3, which noticed it threatens the *proven* Lemma 1 directly (not only the unproven Lemma 2, as might be assumed); carried unchanged through Document 5; in Document 6, noted (informally, not proven) that Sound Local Approximation appears to scale to a changing node set more gracefully than the other two Branch-2 remediation candidates - a claim Document 7 then made precise and proved for the no-reference-change case (Lemma S1). Still fully open in general - the paper's own system model fixes a finite `N` with no discussion of nodes joining or leaving.

**B. M1 vs. M2 compatibility mode** (whether `R_i`/`Omega_i`/`Psi_i` can see causal metadata at all). Raised in Document 1 as an open design choice; identified in Document 3 as one of Lemma 2's four missing prerequisites; in Document 5, sharpened to a factual finding rather than an open choice - the real paper's own `Accept(...)` formula takes no clock argument, so the paper as given is Mode-M1-shaped (causally blind) by construction. Whether a Mode-M2 extension should be pursued remains open.

**C. Merge/conflict-resolution semantics for concurrent updates.** Raised in Document 1, carried as an unresolved Lemma-2 prerequisite in Document 3, restated in Document 5 as still unaddressed by the paper (the Async Drift Compensator resolves detected conflicts by rollback-to-anchor, not by a stated merge rule). Not revisited in Documents 6 or 7.

**D. The actual logical content (not just the type signature) of `R_i`/`Omega_i`/`Psi_i`.** Raised in Document 1 as unknown; the entire subject of Document 4's investigation, which found no internal MoCKA source and left it formally unresolved pending external access; resolved by Document 2 at the level of the paper's own type signatures and Local Invariant Gate formula. Document 5 then showed (Section 3.1 there) this cannot be resolved further *in general*, because the paper deliberately leaves `Omega_i`/`Psi_i` as free parameters of the framework - any given deployment's actual content is a per-deployment fact the paper does not and cannot fix once and for all. Documents 6 and 7 both work with this same understanding throughout.

**E. Whether the real paper (beyond what the available transcription captured) already contains material this Track A series is unknowingly reconstructing or diverging from** - in particular, an explicit multi-node composition theorem, or an explicit requirement resembling Document 6's Factorization/Coherent-Overlap conditions. Raised in Document 5 (Section 5.1, item 2) and again in Document 6 (Section 5, item 6). Not resolved in either document; this consolidation does not resolve it either.

**F. Transcription fidelity of specific notation.** Flagged directly in Document 2's own "Transcription-doubtful spots" (the `sqsubseteq_{Ri}` refinement symbol; whether Assumption 4's "`Omega_i = Omega_j`" is literal function equality or a per-argument biconditional; the two figure descriptions being paraphrases, not extracted captions) and carried into Document 5 (Section 5.1, item 4). Unresolved in every document that mentions it.

**G. Whether the Async Drift Compensator actually tests Assumption 4, or only something related to it.** Raised only in Document 5 (Section 5.1, item 3): the Drift Compensator is described in terms of trace/observation-equivalence drift, not in terms of directly comparing two nodes' invariant outputs, and Document 5 explicitly declined to treat these as the same check. Not revisited in Documents 6 or 7.

**H. Whether the paper requires (anywhere beyond what this transcription captured) that `Omega_i`/`Psi_i` be derived from one shared policy, i.e. whether Branch 1's discipline is ever mandated rather than merely permitted.** Raised only in Document 5 (Section 5.1, item 1). Not resolved.

**I. Lemma B (Global Trace Existence / Lifting) itself.** The central open item of Document 6 (Section 2.5). Neither proven nor disproven; no counterexample was attempted in either direction. Document 7 confirmed explicitly (its Section 3.3) that Sound Local Approximation does not touch this gap at all - it remains exactly as open as Document 6 left it.

**J. Whether Document 6's Condition C (Coherent Overlap) follows from Assumptions 1-3, or is a genuinely independent requirement.** Raised only in Document 6 (Section 5, item 2). Document 6 introduces Condition C as a new hypothesis and does not show it follows from anything already in the paper; not resolved.

**K. Whether Sound Local Approximation is an adequate substitute for Assumption 4's literal equality, or whether the equality does load-bearing work not yet identified.** Raised in Document 6 (Section 5, items 3-4). Document 7 sharpened this considerably (its Section 3.2): it showed precisely *why* Sound Local Approximation is not a substitute for Lemma A - two independently-sound nodes can still disagree on a shared observation without either being wrong - rather than merely flagging the question as open. Whether that weaker guarantee is nonetheless adequate for whatever practical purpose motivates wanting compositional safety in the first place remains unresolved.

**L. Definition of `E_i` ("causally relevant events") and the history-horizon/pruning policy for the deferred Candidate C.** Raised in Document 1 (Open Questions 3 and 6) in the context of the from-scratch `pi_i^causal` formalism. Neither item was carried forward explicitly into Documents 5-7, which work directly in the real paper's own state/trace-level formalism rather than the `E_i`-based one Document 1 used before the paper was available. This consolidation does not resolve either item, but notes - without asserting it as settled - that they may have been effectively superseded once the real paper's own apparatus became the working reference, since the real paper does not use an `E_i` construct at all.

**M. Relationship to Track B (MoCKA implementation).** Raised in Document 1 (Open Question 8) as a question of whether this theoretical work should ever inform or be informed by Track B's own event-ordering mechanisms. Never resolved in any of the first six documents, each of which independently maintained the same non-crossing restraint this consolidation also maintains. Document 7 was the first task to deliberately approach this boundary, under explicit separate authorization, but did so only at the level of a purely conditional mapping (its Section 4, properties C1-C4) - it asserts nothing about MoCKA's actual state, so this item is narrowed, not resolved: whether MoCKA's real Governance Function `G` satisfies any of Document 7's stated conditions remains fully open, and is explicitly left to Human Gate or a separate Track B task.

**N. How to recover Lemma S2 under a strengthening reference update.** New, from Document 7 (its Section 2.2): Document 7 proved soundness survives a *weakening* update automatically and gave a concrete counterexample showing it does not survive a *strengthening* update without re-verification, but did not address what recovery would look like in the strengthening case - whether every node genuinely needs full re-proof, or whether some partial reuse of the prior proof is possible (e.g. only re-checking the specific region of the state space the strengthening actually affects), is not explored anywhere in this series.

**O. Whether an integrated framework exists treating the Branch 1/Branch 2 axis and the Sound Local Approximation axis together, or whether they must be handled as separate, non-interacting concerns.** New, arising directly from Document 7's central repositioning (Section 3.4 there, "a third, independent axis"). Document 7 characterizes the two as answering different questions but does not propose, or investigate the existence of, any single framework that would let a system reason about both simultaneously (for instance, whether Sound Local Approximation could be combined with a partial, weaker form of Condition F/C to recover some fraction of Lemma A's cross-node agreement without its full cost). Not explored.

**P. Branch 2's actual node-disagreement problem remains fully unevaluated beyond Document 6's initial identification.** Stated explicitly here per this update's instructing memo: of the three Branch-2 remediation candidates Document 6 named (synchronous cross-node verification gate, pairwise onboarding contracts, Sound Local Approximation), only the third has been developed in any depth (Document 7) - and Document 7's own finding is that this third candidate does not solve the disagreement problem the other two were meant to address. The synchronous cross-node gate and pairwise onboarding contracts remain exactly at the level of description and rough cost/coverage comparison Document 6 gave them (its Section 3.1-3.2 and the summary table in 3.4); neither has been formalized or evaluated further by any subsequent document. Branch 2 as a problem - can independently-defined node predicates ever be reconciled without one of these costlier mechanisms - is therefore still open, not merely under-explored.
